### The scale question, answered before it is asked

All 327 parts running at once was measured, not assumed: 59 parts cost 2.05 of 12
cores and 2.08 of 29.4 GiB, so 327 at the same per-part cost is **11.4 cores and
11.5 GiB** — and that is an upper bound, because the 59 running are the busiest
path and an idle part with an empty inbox costs far less.

It fits. But **RL-072 does not mean everything is switched on and left there** —
that would take the switch from the governor, which T-2 says is the only part
allowed to hold it. Each phase switches its block on, proves it carries, and then
the governor decides what stays on.

## Phase 5 — closed-trade decoding (20 parts)

**Why first.** 115 round trips have closed and nothing has scored one. Every part
in this block reads `closed-trade` and `peak-excursion`, both of which are already
being produced and journalled. It is the largest block whose entire input is
already live, and its outputs are what makes the next trade better rather than
merely different.

**Parts:** `pnl-attributor`, `loss-cause-classifier`, `exit-quality-scorer`,
`entry-quality-scorer`, `stop-placement-auditor`, `excursion-profiler`,
`shortfall-decomposer`, `exit-counterfactual-replayer`, `luck-skill-separator`,
`exploration-pair-decoder`, and the rest of the 20.

**The Instagram rule this block inherits, and it is a hard one.** From the
three-tier memory guide (`docs/research/instagram-2026-08-20.md` §2): *embed the
symptom, not the fix*. An episode must be keyed on **the market condition that was
observed**, never on the outcome. Storing "lost 2% on a long" retrieves nothing
later; storing "price jumped 4% in three minutes on rising volume after a funding
flip" is what a future scan can match against. The same source makes the record
**append-only and written on resolution** — which is what a closed trade already
is.

**Also inherited:** the Drake-equation decomposition (§6). Expectancy is not one
number, it is a chain each of whose factors is separately measurable:

    E[trade] = P(signal real) × P(entry fills) × P(target before stop) × avg win
             − (1 − P(target before stop)) × avg loss
             − costs

The point is not the product. It is that the product tells you **which factor** is
sinking expectancy. `pnl-attributor` and `luck-skill-separator` are where it lives.

**Repaired first:** the 10 closed trades already in the journal carrying entry
prices the market never printed. They predate the 2026-08-25 fix and must be
marked as such rather than scored — a decoder that trains on them learns from a
bug.

**Done when:** 20 of 20 running, every `closed-trade` and `peak-excursion` wire
carrying, and a scored round trip visible on the board with its attribution.

## Phase 6 — prediction (16 parts)

**Why second.** It is the `forecast` column the trade board has rendered as

**Parts:** `kronos-forecaster`, `kronos-finetuner`, `kronos-size-selector`,
`realised-vol-regressor`, `entropy-magnitude-forecaster`, `funding-rate-forecaster`,
`forecast-ensembler`, `forecast-scorer`, `forecast-distribution-gate`,
`model-drift-monitor`, `liquidation-cluster-mapper`, and the rest.

**Kronos is already recorded and is not a new decision.** `upstream_dependencies`
carries it, verified via `gh` rather than from the post: decoder-only foundation
model for K-line sequences, Tsinghua, AAAI 2026, MIT, model zoo of mini (4.1M,
context 2048), small (24.7M), base (102.3M) — and **Kronos-large (499.2M) is not
open-source**, which is why `kronos-size-selector` exists at all.

### The gap this phase closes, named because it was found by looking

`realised-vol-regressor` is built on HAR-style features — `close_to_close_short`,
`close_to_close_long`, Parkinson, Garman-Klass, a short/long ratio,
implied-over-realised, largest gap fraction. That is a defensible design and it is
**not** the specification the volatility reel actually gave
(`instagram-2026-08-20.md` §1), which was ten named features with formulas.

Four of the missing ones need nothing this system does not already have, and were
dropped without a reason being written down:

| feature | formula | why it is not covered by what is there |
|---|---|---|
| downside variance, 5d | `Σ_{rᵢ<0} rᵢ²` | close-to-close is symmetric; this separates vol that came from sell-offs |
| jump variance | `RV − BV`, `BV = (π/2)Σ\|rᵢ\|\|rᵢ₋₁\|` | Parkinson and Garman-Klass use the range; neither separates a jump from diffusion |
| vol-of-vol, 20d | `σ(RV over t−19:t)` | a steady 40% and a 20%→60% traverse are not the same 40% |
| `RV₂₂` | `√(252·(1/22)Σ variance)` | HAR needs a long leg; only short and long exist and long is not 22d |

Three more — ATM IV₃₀, term slope `IV₃₀ − IV₆₀`, put skew `IV₂₅Δ − IV_ATM` —
need an options surface this project does not have. That limit was recorded
honestly at the time and stands; the four above have no such excuse.

**Also from that reel, and already honoured:** trade *how much* it moves, not
which way. `realised-vol-regressor`'s own docstring reaches the same conclusion
independently — returns are close to unpredictable and their magnitude is not.

**`forecast-scorer` gets a stated method**, which it has never had. From the
neural-network sheet (§3): walk-forward validation, time-based splits, no
look-ahead, and **out-of-sample evaluation with transaction costs**. A forecast
scored without costs is scored against a market nobody trades in.

**Done when:** 16 of 16 running, a forecast on the board's open-position table,
and `forecast-scorer` reporting accuracy that was measured out of sample.

## Phase 8 — risk, capital desk, and the operator's controls (16 parts)

**This is where every capital ruling lands**, and there are more of them than any
other subject in the project's record.

**Parts:** `leverage-selector`, `capital-allotment-reader`,
`main-account-settings-reader`, `capital-settings-validator`,
`capital-settings-change-recorder`, `capital-utilisation-meter`,
`allocation-conservation-checker`, `allocation-rebalance-proposer`,
`live-balance-divergence-watch`, `paper-currency-converter`, `drawdown-breaker`,
`stop-frequency-breaker`, `margin-liquidation-watch`, `profit-lock`,
`liquidation-price-tracker`, `size-hint-writer`.

### What the operator can see and change

| ruling | requirement |
|---|---|
| RL-040 | editable total paper capital, per-bot cap, min and max per trade |
| RL-041 | leverage chosen per trade — 5x, 10x, 20x — under a ceiling |
| RL-051 | main balance, paper currency, per-segment allocation, min/max, leverage, **for every segment bot** |
| RL-053 | leverage per bot is a **ceiling**; the bot still chooses under it from volatility plus funding |
| RL-054 | an order below the minimum is **bumped up** to it, not dropped — unless the risk limit forbids, and then the limit wins |
| RL-028 | every segment bot shows USDT capital used and USDT profit or loss |
| RL-055 | the board shows current values **and when each last changed**, read from `capital-settings-change-recorder`'s journal — never the file's mtime, never `git log` |

### The three rules the operator added on 2026-08-25

1. **On live, capital is not editable.** The board *displays* the real balance
   read from the wallet or broker. An editable number that disagrees with the
   venue is a number that will size a trade the account cannot pay for.
2. **Min, max and leverage have system-chosen defaults** until the operator
   overrides them.
3. **Those defaults scale with measured bot accuracy** — more size on setups the
   bull or bear bot has been right about, less on unsure ones. This is not a new
   mechanism: `size-hint-writer` already exists to *"restate a conviction as a
   capped fraction of capital the sizer may use"*, and `bot-scorecard` is where
   the measured accuracy comes from. The default is a function of the scorecard,
   and it is refused rather than guessed while the scorecard is empty.

### The write path, and why it is the most dangerous thing in this plan

RL-055 decided settings are edited in a file over SSH and the board only displays
them, and the capital-desk proposal hardened that into *"the dashboard is a data
producer, never a control switch"*. `main-account.toml` says no part can write it
by construction (R-01). On 2026-08-25 the operator asked for them editable in the
dashboard, which reverses that — **and the premise changed underneath it**: RL-055
assumed an SSH tunnel, and the board is now on a public URL.

So the write path is built with all of this, and a phase that ships any of it
without the rest has not finished the phase:

- **Password-gated**, against the scrypt hash already recorded outside every repo
  (`dashboard/board_password.py`, set 2026-08-25).
- **Rate-limited at the door, widening on each failure.** A thirteen-character
  password is strong against a person and weak against a machine allowed to guess
  without limit.
- **Validated before it lands.** Every write goes through
  `capital-settings-validator`; an inconsistent set zeroes the risk limit rather
  than trading on a guess.
- **Journalled.** `capital-settings-change-recorder` writes which value, old, new,
  when — and that journal is the only source for the board's "last changed".
- **The board writes the file; no part does.** R-01 is untouched, because the
  board is off-diagram substrate and not a part.
- **The live switch is separate and louder than everything else.** RL-005 makes
  paper-first the method; `live-switch-guard` already issues a zero risk limit
  until the bots graduate, so a premature switch produces a live segment that
  places no orders rather than one that places bad ones.

**Done when:** 16 of 16 running, every value above visible on the board with its
last-changed time, an edit round-tripping through validate → journal → file, and
`leverage-selector` choosing under the ceiling on a real trade.

## Phase 15 — backtesting and autonomous operation (27 parts)

**Backtesting exists because the Instagram harvest exposed its absence.** Five of
the ten repositories in the first carousel were backtesting frameworks and this
blueprint had no backtesting block; it was raised as a gap, and the block is now
10 declared parts. `instruction-promotion-gate` is the point of it: an instruction
reaches the scanner only after surviving replay.

**RL-071 governs the boundary and it is absolute.** A replay is never what a
trading decision or a live run's learning is made from. Backtesting proves an
instruction *before* the scanner is told to watch for it; the bot still trades on
prices as they arrive.

**Autonomous (17 parts) is last on purpose.** `part-admission-gate` admits a
part the system proposed itself. Nothing should be able to add parts to this
system until every other block has been observed running.

**Done when:** 27 of 27 running, an instruction promoted through replay, and the
coverage probe reading **327 of 327 parts and 5,012 of 5,012 wires**.

## What this plan does not do

- **No spot, no options** (RL-050, RL-062). Futures first, fully. Those segments
  stay honestly empty, and phases for them are written when futures is done.
- **No raising the symbol count.** RL-072's sibling instruction from the same day:
  hold at 50 per venue until the project is 100% built. The walk was stepped up
  twice on clean short measurements and both times the cost appeared hours later.
- **No live money** until `live-switch-guard` graduates the bots on paper results:
  enough closed trades, positive after costs, drawdown inside tolerance, enough
  elapsed time — all four, because each alone is gameable.
- **No promise that a full board is a good bot.** Coverage is the goal RL-072
  set, and it is a goal about honesty rather than about profit. Whether the
  numbers are worth anything is what phase 5 exists to answer, and it is a
  separate question that this plan does not pretend to settle.

## How results get reported

From OmniPhi's own honest-assessment panel, which was the one item in six posts
that named where it loses (`instagram-2026-08-20.md` §5): state what the strategy
**does not** do, by how much, and why the sample is too short to trust. Every
other post in that set showed a rising equity curve and a win rate. This project
reports the first way. It is Rule 8 applied to performance rather than to a tile.
